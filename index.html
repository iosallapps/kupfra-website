Construction and electrical calculator for iPhone

Stairs, cables and concrete.

One toolkit for electricians, builders and DIY: a stair designed in 3D, the right cable and breaker, and the materials for the job, with the working shown.

Free to start. Unlock everything with a subscription or once for life.

Three modules, one app.

Stairs in 3D

Enter the floor to floor rise and the room you have, and Kupfra lays out the stair: number of risers, riser height, tread run, pitch and step length.

  • Straight, L-shaped, U-shaped, quarter winder, half winder and spiral
  • Cut list, materials and cost
  • Client PDF with your logo

Electrical

Size the cable and check the protection, following IEC 60364, BS 7671 for the UK or the NEC for the US.

  • Cable size and voltage drop
  • Breaker and fuse selection
  • Short circuit, earthing, conduit fill, line sizing and Ohm's law

Construction

The materials for the job, with adhesive, grout, mortar and waste worked out.

  • Concrete and screed, tiles, paint
  • Drywall, bricks and blocks, insulation
  • Flooring and roofing

A stair you can build from.

  • Checked against your guidance

    Every value is compared with the guidance you choose: DIN 18065, the US IRC or the English Approved Document K. Headroom is checked against the ceiling opening, and each check names the rule it comes from.

  • Realistic 3D

    See the stair in oak, beech, walnut, concrete or steel, with closed or cut stringers or a steel spine, and wood, metal, glass or horizontal rail balustrades. Drag to turn it, pinch to zoom.

  • Cut list and materials

    The cut list gives the stringer marks for each flight, the treads, landings and risers, ready to share as text or PDF. The materials and cost screen adds board areas, volumes and waste, priced with your own unit prices in your currency.

  • A client PDF with your logo

    Turn a design into a proposal: cover with the 3D render, plan and side elevation, dimensions, cut list, materials and cost, your notes and the client's signature, under your company name, contact details and logo.

  • Measure with the camera

    Point the camera at the floor and the floor above to measure the rise, or across the opening to measure its length, and use the value straight away. Devices with LiDAR use scene depth.

  • Projects

    Save a stair or a calculation as a project and pick it up later. Projects stay on your device.

The working, on screen.

Your region, your language.

Kupfra comes in 45 languages and follows your device. Units are metric or imperial, set once, and the stair guidance and electrical standard are the ones you pick: IEC 60364 or BS 7671 in Europe and the UK, the NEC in the US, copper or aluminum, PVC or XLPE, by installation method.

The results are guidance for planning. Check your local building code before you build, and have electrical work signed off by a qualified electrician. Kupfra does not replace the code, an engineer or an inspector.

Questions

Is Kupfra free?

Yes to start. The straight stair, the cable size calculator and the concrete and screed calculator are free, with up to 3 saved projects, and the free version shows ads. The unlock adds every stair type, every calculator, design options, the materials and cost estimate, client PDFs, PDF and text export, camera measuring and unlimited projects, and removes the ads.

How do I unlock it?

With a weekly or monthly auto-renewing subscription, or once with a lifetime purchase. The app shows each price in your currency before you buy, and a yearly plan may also be offered in the app.

Which standards does it follow?

Electrical results follow IEC 60364, BS 7671 for the UK or the NEC for the US. Stair values are compared with the guidance you choose: DIN 18065, the US IRC or the English Approved Document K. Each result names the table or rule it comes from.

Can I trust the results on site?

Treat them as guidance for planning. Check your local building code before you build, and have electrical work signed off by a qualified electrician. Kupfra shows its working so you can check it, but it does not replace the code, an engineer or an inspector.

How accurate is camera measuring?

The app tells you as you measure: usually within 2 to 5 cm, and within about 1 cm on devices with LiDAR. Check with a tape before you cut. The camera is used only to measure, on your device. Nothing is recorded or saved.

Which languages and units?

45 languages, following your device's language. Units are metric or imperial, set once in Settings, and start from your device's region.

Which devices?

iPhone. Camera measuring needs a device that supports ARKit world tracking, and uses LiDAR depth where the device has it.
