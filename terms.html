2. What Kupfra does

Kupfra is a toolkit for electricians, builders and DIY: a 3D stair designer with cut list, materials, cost estimate and client PDF, eight electrical calculators (cable size, voltage drop, breaker selection, short-circuit current, earthing, conduit fill, line sizing, Ohm's law) following IEC 60364, BS 7671 or the NEC, eight construction calculators (concrete, tiles, paint, drywall, masonry, insulation, flooring, roof) and camera measuring of stair rise and opening.

6. Important: results and your responsibility

Every result in Kupfra is an estimate for planning, not a design, a certificate or an approval. Kupfra checks stair values against the guidance you pick (DIN 18065, the US IRC or the English Approved Document K) and electrical results against the tables of IEC 60364, BS 7671 or the NEC. These tables are a reference and can be out of date, simplified or not the rule that applies to your site: if your country's rules are not built in, Kupfra says so and uses a reference table. The building code, wiring regulations and rules that apply where you work always decide, and so does the authority that enforces them. Check your local building code before you build.

Never use Kupfra as the only basis for electrical work. Cable size, voltage drop, breaker selection, short-circuit current, earthing and conduit fill are guides only: the installation must be designed, carried out, inspected and tested by a qualified, licensed electrician in line with local regulations, such as BS 7671 and Part P of the Building Regulations in England and Wales, the NEC and your local amendments in the United States, or IEC 60364 and your national rules. Kupfra is not an inspection, testing or certification tool and its results are not an electrical sign-off. Measured values on site, such as earth loop impedance and insulation resistance, always override a calculated one. Wrong electrical work can cause fire, electric shock and death.

Kupfra is not structural engineering. Do not use it to size beams, floors, openings, supports, foundations or any load-bearing part, or as the sole basis for a stair, guard or handrail that people will use. Stairs, landings, balustrades and openings must meet the building code and be checked by a qualified builder, structural engineer or building control before they are built or used; a falling or collapsing stair can kill.

Camera measuring is an estimate: usually within 2 to 5 cm, and within about 1 cm on devices with LiDAR. Check every measurement with a tape before you cut or order. Quantities, waste, cost estimates and cut lists depend on the values and unit prices you enter and on the product you buy; check product data sheets and local regulations before you order or build. A client PDF is a proposal made from your inputs, not a certified drawing, a quotation with legal effect or a code-compliance statement, and you are responsible for what you give a client.
